Try it on a USB stick first

A normal Mint stick starts with a US keyboard. A normal Zorin stick asks for a language first, but has no Belgian choice: Nederlands gives you a US keyboard, Français a French one. It stays that way until you have installed Linux. On our stick, the boot menu first asks for your language (Nederlands, Français, Deutsch, English) and then your keyboard: Belgian AZERTY with a period or a comma on the numeric keypad. Our packages are already on it. If you install from the stick, they come along.

This is a stopgap. It is needed until Mint and Zorin have a Belgian choice in their own boot menu. It is not our own version of Mint or Zorin, and we are not affiliated with those projects.

How do you make it? You download the official ISO yourself, from linuxmint.com or zorin.com. Our tool changes only the boot menu, the list of checksums, one extra file with our packages, and the /belgian/ folder. Everything else is the original system.

  • On Linux (or with WSL on Windows): follow the recipe in the README, under "Live USB".
  • With Windows and Rufus only: in preparation. The first test in the emulator passed. A test with a real stick is still to be done.

Rather not use a stick? That works too. Install Mint or Zorin as usual, then follow the install page. You only miss the Belgian keyboard during the first few minutes.
